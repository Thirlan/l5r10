Law in Rokugan

Law in Rokugan is inseparable from the Emperor, the Celestial Order, and the duties of each social station. Imperial law provides a common framework, while clan and local authorities administer justice within their own jurisdictions.

Foundations of Law

Government and Imperial Authority

The Emperor is regarded as the final authority on law. The Empire belongs to the Emperor, whose mandate comes from the Celestial Heavens; an Imperial command therefore carries both political and religious weight. The Imperial government delegates authority through its bureaucracy, champions, magistrates, governors, and daimyo.

This authority can be abused. Rokugani history includes periods when emperors, ministers, or magistrates used legal power for personal or political ends. The ideal of law is therefore balanced against duty, honor, precedent, and the practical limits of enforcement.

The Celestial Order

The Celestial Order is the religious and social model that assigns every soul a place in the Celestial Pattern. It is expressed through the major castes of samurai, clergy, and bonge, with hinin regarded as outside the recognized social order. The samurai hierarchy itself places the Emperor above the kuge, buke, ronin, and ashigaru.

A person's dharma is the duty associated with their station. How well that duty is performed affects karma and the possibility of a higher station after reincarnation. Defying the order is consequently treated as both a social offense and a spiritual failure, although Shinsei's teachings hold that training and enlightenment can allow a person to surpass the limits imposed by society.

Ritsuryo

Ritsuryo is the Empire's fundamental system of penal and administrative law. It was associated with Otomo Kuchiru and the legal thinking of Soshi Saibankan, and it traditionally gives great weight to testimony, honor, and confession rather than physical evidence.

The law is reviewed at the annual Hashira Horitsu, or Pillars of Law, a conference of magistrates, courtiers, judges, and nobles held during the Month of the Dragon. The Emperor may overrule the conference, while the Emerald Champion presides when the Emperor is absent.

Articles of Heaven

The Articles of Heaven were written in 622 by Seppun Hanako to help the young Hantei XVII restore the Emperor's reputation. They were broad legal reforms covering the treatment of prisoners and hostages, the use of torture, and the quality of life of heimin. They established stricter standards for lawful custody and punishment.

Imperial Edicts

An Imperial edict, or Imperial decree, is an order of the Emperor and is treated as inviolate. Edicts can change the structure of society, create or dissolve clans and families, prohibit practices, define religious policy, or impose restrictions across the whole Empire. The Department of Edicts is responsible for ensuring that such commands are promulgated throughout Rokugan.

Examples include restrictions on clan warfare, the creation of minor clans, bans on gaijin contact and gunpowder after the Battle of White Stag, and the prohibition of opium except for medical purposes in the tenth century.

Imperial Institutions

Ministry of Justice

The Ministry of Justice maintains legal records and manages the administrative side of punishment. It tracks fines, incarceration, and other penalties, providing bureaucratic support to magistrates rather than replacing their investigative or judicial authority.

Department of Edicts

The Department of Edicts belongs to the Ministry of Proclamations and Protocols. Its role is to distribute Imperial decrees effectively so that governors, daimyo, magistrates, and local officials know which commands are in force.

Court of Religious Affairs and Festivals

This court was a subordinate body of the Ministry of Service. It reviewed religious matters and created Imperial religious edicts. It was traditionally led by a Seppun, although members of the Kitsu, Isawa, Agasha, Tamori, or Asahina families could serve when no suitable Seppun was available.

Hitojichi Laws

The Hitojichi, or Hostage, Laws were drafted by Hantei XI after the alliance against Iuchiban. Each daimyo was required to spend at least one month of every year in Otosan Uchi. After the daimyo returned home, members of the daimyo's family remained in the Imperial capital for another month as hostages, reinforcing the Emperor's control over the clans.

Magistrates and Enforcement

Magistrates

A magistrate enforces the laws of a province, family, clan, or the Empire. Clan magistrates enforce clan law and may serve as judges, circuit riders, investigators, or supervisors. Emerald Magistrates enforce Imperial law, while Jade Magistrates investigate crimes involving magic and the arcane.

Emerald Magistrates

The Emerald Magistrates serve under the Emerald Champion and, through that office, the Emperor. They were founded in 153 by Doji Hatsuo and Soshi Saibankan alongside the Imperial Legions. The Emerald Magistrates were trained at the Palace of the Emerald Champion and carried a jade orb as their symbol of office.

Charter of the Emerald Magistrate

The Charter defines the jurisdiction, duties, rights, and limits of Emerald Magistrates.

  • Jurisdiction: Imperial crimes, national crimes, Shadowlands incursions, cross-border fugitives, blasphemous rituals, and acts that threaten civil disorder.
  • Duties: Enforce Imperial law, oversee tax collection, authorize travel papers, protect dignitaries crossing clan borders, report to the Emerald Champion, and refer cases outside their jurisdiction to the proper authority.
  • Rights: Question people of lower station, sentence a confessed criminal, compel the appearance of a higher-ranking person with a valid order, request information from governors and daimyo, and commandeer troops within the charter's limits.
  • Restrictions: They do not perform routine street patrols, investigate ordinary local crimes, approve or invalidate blood feuds, command troops without the required notice or authorization, or accept payment for official duties.

Yoriki

Yoriki are the chief assistants to magistrates. They investigate crimes, find and record witness testimony, handle prisoners, conduct questioning, oversee doshin, and inspect travel papers at waystations. They prepare cases and enforce the law but generally do not render the final judgment.

Doshin

Doshin are the lowest level of formal law enforcement. They assist a yoriki or a magistrate's deputy, deal with minor crimes, and exercise authority in a village and its surroundings. A doshin is often chosen locally with the advice of the village headman; in areas lacking suitable samurai, a heimin may serve.

Budoka

Budoka are armed peasants who have adopted Bushido and trained in combat, especially unarmed fighting. Doshin may select them as deputies. Their authority is limited, usually involving minor offenses and the eta, but they give local law enforcement additional manpower.

Travel Papers

Travelers crossing clan borders are generally expected to carry and present travel papers. Yoriki and other officials inspect these documents at waystations, and Emerald Magistrates can issue papers with Imperial authority and a broader scope.

Courts and Procedure

Court

Courts are convened by a chief magistrate, or by a daimyo for purely local matters. The judge is responsible for the sentence, while the question of guilt is normally settled before the accused reaches court. A court complex usually includes a courtroom, records and legal texts, scribes, and quarters for yoriki and doshin.

In a formal courtroom, the accused kneels on the white sands of judgment while the scribe reads the charges and the confession. The judge may ask clarifying questions before issuing a final sentence, which is normally carried out immediately.

Torture and Confession

Traditional Rokugani procedure expects an accused person to provide a confession before trial. If a confession is not offered voluntarily, yoriki may request torture, usually through eta deputies who are trained for the task. A magistrate is expected to authorize this method, and the yoriki must present a reasoned explanation for why it is necessary.

The Articles of Heaven placed limits on this practice and improved protections for prisoners. Torture remains a grim and controversial part of the legal system: it can produce a confession, but it also creates opportunities for abuse, coercion, and false conclusions.

Kitsuki Method

The Kitsuki Method is an investigative approach founded by Agasha Kitsuki and developed by the Kitsuki family. It relies on careful observation, physical evidence, perception, awareness, and intuition rather than treating testimony as the sole path to truth. Kitsuki magistrates and some Emerald Magistrates use it as an alternative to the confession-centered traditions of Ritsuryo.

Toshiyori-koe

The Toshiyori-koe, or Old Voices, were a movement of Kitsu, shugenja, and monks who argued that spirits and ancestors should be allowed to give testimony in legal proceedings just as living witnesses did. The proposal reflects the tension between spiritual truth, ancestor veneration, and the practical rules of Imperial courts.

Hajisherazu

Hajisherazu were guardians of the prisons at Otosan Uchi. They were drawn from inmates and retired samurai seeking redemption through prison service.

Special Legal Customs

Blood Feud

A blood feud is a legal vendetta, usually declared after a samurai is killed needlessly or seriously wronged. The victim and immediate family are the ordinary participants. To make the feud legal, both families' daimyo must provide written consent. Once recognized, the feud is treated like a declaration of war against the offending party, and outsiders generally avoid intervention unless they are willing to damage their own honor.

Law and Social Station

Law is applied through the Celestial Order. Rank affects who may command, who may be questioned, which court has jurisdiction, and what forms of redress are available. The same action can carry different consequences depending on the status of the people involved, the authority of the official, and whether the matter concerns local, clan, Imperial, religious, or supernatural law.
