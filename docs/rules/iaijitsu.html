Iaijitsu Duels

Overview

An Iaijitsu duel is a formalized combat between two duelists where each ring plays a role in different action options. The duel consists of 2 Phases, the Preparation phase and the Strike phase.

It is important to note that Void can be used as normal. Hence it is highly recommended to consider using it to increase dice rolls, increase armor, reduce damage taken or increase damage inflicted (if it is a duel to the death).

Preparation Phase

The Preparation phase involves two rounds each with sub-steps: Action Selection and Resolution. Both duelists go through these steps in each round, and once done with the 2nd round proceed to the Strike phase.

Action Selection Step

Each character chooses ONE of the following actions that they have not chosen in a previous round, then reveal simultaneously:

ActionSkillRingBenefit
FocusMeditationVoidGain +5 to your Iaijitsu roll
AssessBattleWaterLearn 2 traits or skills of your opponent and opposing character declares what two actions they will NOT pick next round (You cannot declare a previously used action).
ReadinessKenjutsuFireGain +5 to your hit roll
DodgeDefenseAirGain +5 to your armor TN
EndureMeditationEarthIgnore all wound and status effect penalties (e.g. fatigue) for the remainder of the duel and subsequent skirmish if it is a duel to the death. In addition you automatically succeed on all Willpower rolls to avoid screaming in pain from damage.

Resolution Step

  • Both duelists roll the skill / ring for their chosen actions
  • Higher roll of the two chosen actions gets their benefit
  • In the event of a tie:
    • Check which element has advantage according to the elemental cycle:
      • Fire wins against Air
      • Air wins against Water
      • Water wins against Earth
      • Earth wins against Fire
      • Void loses against all other elements
      • All other combinations are a tie
    • If there is still a tie then the draw is decided in order of
      • Highest Ring
      • Highest Skill
      • Highest Insight Rank
    • If there is still a tie then it is a draw and neither side get their benefits.

Withdrawal Phase

Either duelist in this phase may choose to withdraw from the duel, conceding victory to the opponent. If neither do it proceeds to the Striking Phase. If this was a duel to the death, the withdrawing duelist is expected to commit seppuku.

Striking Phase

The striking phase is composed of two different rounds:

  • Initiative round
  • Attack round

Initiative round

Both duelists attempt to draw their swords with the winner being the one to draw it fastest.

  • Both roll Iaijutsu/Reflexes and add any bonuses
  • If difference < 5: it is a kharmic strike and both strike simultaneously
  • If difference ≥ 5: Higher roller strikes first

Attack Round

In the attack round, the duelists are trying to see if their strikes hit and damage the opponent.

  • If there was a winner last round then they attack first
  • They roll Kenjutsu/Reflexes and add any bonuses
  • If they beat the other Duelist's armor TN then they deal damage as normal
  • The damaged duelist must make a Willpower + Honor roll against a TN equal to the penalty of the wound rank to see if they commit a minor breach of etiquette by screaming in pain (e.g. Nicked is +3 so a TN of 3, but Crippled is a +20 so a 20 TN).
  • The next duelist then takes their turn to strike, following the same procedure
  • If it was a kharmic strike then both duelists strike simultaneously

Conclusion

If the duel is to the death, then the duel becomes a normal skirmish between the combatants. Otherwise the duel ends and the victor is determined.

Victory is determined by whomever drew first blood. If it was a kharmic strike with both hitting and dealing damage then the duel is considered a draw, regardless of the damage dealt or if one of the duelists died.

Iaijitsu Dueling Etiquette

There are various rules of etiquette that govern Iaijitsu duels and failing to follow them can result in varying consequences ranging from breaches of etiquette to disqualification of the duelists if these were sanctioned duels.

  • Duels are expected to be done using Katanas
  • Armor is not worn during duels
  • Permission must be sought by your lords to engage in duels
  • If not a duel to the death, the duel ends when first blood is drawn
  • Killing an opponent in a non-lethal duel is considered a grave breach of etiquette

It is not uncommon for unsanctioned duels to occur, though these are generally frowned upon. There are many stories of infamous duels occurring where many of the rules of etiquette were thrown to the wind, such as the duel between Hida Yakamo and Mirumoto Satsu where the crab samurai used a Tetsubo and killed Mirumoto Satsu, all over a minor insult.

Example Duels

The following examples demonstrate how Iaijitsu duels are played out in practice. Each example includes narrative context, dice rolls, and the mechanical resolution of the duel.

Example 1: Kakita Suzumi vs Akodo Keio - Non-Lethal Duel

Setup: Kakita Suzumi, a young Crane duelist, has accepted a challenge from Akodo Keio, an ambitious Lion warrior eager to make a name for himself. The two stand in the dojo at dawn, their katanas sheathed, tensions high.

Round 1 - Preparation Phase

Action Selection: Suzumi chooses Focus. Keio chooses Assess.

Resolution: Suzumi rolls Meditation/Void: 5k2 = 12. Keio rolls Battle/Water: 4k2 = 9. Suzumi wins and gains +5 to her Iaijitsu strike roll.

Round 2 - Preparation Phase

Action Selection: Suzumi chooses Readiness. Keio chooses Focus.

Resolution: Suzumi rolls Kenjutsu/Fire: 4k2 = 8. Keio rolls Meditation/Void: 5k3 = 14. Keio wins and gains +5 to his Iaijitsu strike roll.

Withdrawal Phase

Neither duelist withdraws. Both nod respectfully, ready for the strike.

Striking Phase - Initiative Round

Suzumi rolls Iaijutsu/Reflexes + 5 (Focus bonus): 4k3 + 5 = 13. Keio rolls Iaijutsu/Reflexes + 5 (Focus bonus): 3k3 + 5 = 8. Difference is 5 (≥5), so Suzumi strikes first.

Striking Phase - Attack Round

Suzumi's Attack: She rolls Kenjutsu/Agility: 5k3 + 5 = 17. She beats Keio's armor TN of 15. She hits! Damage roll: 5k2 = 25 wounds. Keio cries out in pain as he fails the Willpower + Honor roll: 2k2 + 6 = 11 vs Injured's 15. Blood spills across his shoulder.

Keio's Counter-Attack: Reeling from his wound, Keio still attempts to strike. He rolls Kenjutsu/Agility: 5k2 - 15 (injured) = 6. This fails to beat Suzumi's armor TN of 15. He misses!

Conclusion

Kakita Suzumi is victorious! Keio, despite his confidence, was outmatched by the superior skill of the Crane duelist. He bows deeply, accepting his defeat with grace. Though shamed for crying out and disappointed at his loss, he respects Suzumi's mastery of the blade.

Example 2: Ronin Toku vs Bayushi Sho - Non-Lethal Duel

Setup: Toku, a wandering ronin from Toturi's army, has encountered Bayushi Sho at an inn. A dispute over honor led to harsh words, and now they settle the matter in the traditional way. Sho, confident in Scorpion cunning, believes his preparation will overcome this little samurai.

Round 1 - Preparation Phase

Action Selection: Toku chooses Endure. Sho chooses Focus.

Resolution: Toku rolls Meditation/Earth: 4k3 = 13. Sho rolls Meditation/Void: 4k2 = 10. Toku wins and will ignore all wound penalties for the remainder of the duel and will success on all Willpower checks.

Round 2 - Preparation Phase

Action Selection: Toku chooses Focus. Sho chooses Readiness.

Resolution: Toku rolls Meditation/Void: 5k2 = 11. Sho rolls Kenjutsu/Fire: 4k3 = 12. Sho wins and gains +5 to his hit roll.

Withdrawal Phase

Neither duelist withdraws. The air crackles with tension as both draw their blades.

Striking Phase - Initiative Round

Toku rolls Iaijutsu/Reflexes: 4k2 + 5 = 12. Sho rolls Iaijutsu/Reflexes: 4k3 = 10. Difference is 2 (<5), so this is a Kharmic Strike—both attack simultaneously!

Striking Phase - Attack Round (Simultaneous)

Toku's Attack: Toku rolls Kenjutsu/Agility: 5k3 = 16, beating Sho's armor TN of 15. He hits! Damage: 4k2 = 21 wounds.

Sho's Attack: Sho rolls Kenjutsu/Agility + 5 (Readiness bonus): 4k3 + 5 = 16, beating Toku's armor TN of 11. He hits! Damage: 3k2 = 7 wounds.

Both duelists cry out as they strike each other simultaneously. However, Toku, having chosen Endure, channels his inner strength and does not scream. Toku's superiority of will and body is evident as he stands despite his wound, while Sho writhes in pain from his injury.

Conclusion

The duel is a Draw! Sho bows, respect in his eyes.Despite losing the duel, Toku comes out unscathed. However, Sho's honor is damaged by this outcome, but the two part ways as something resembling friends.

Example 3: Doji Akiko vs Shinjo Amat - Duel to the Death

Setup: High in Tenma Fortress in the recently conquered Yobanjin lands, Shinjo Amat has publicly accused Commander Doji Akiko of conspiring against the Emerald Magistrates themselves. The accusation is not only false but a grave insult to her family's honor. Akiko, outraged, demands satisfaction. Steel will be drawn, and only one will leave this chamber alive. Akiko wields a finely crafted Kakita blade, while Amat carries a jade katana, supposedly blessed by the kami themselves.

Round 1 - Preparation Phase

Action Selection: Akiko chooses Assess. Amat chooses Focus.

Resolution: Akiko rolls Battle/Water: 7k4 = 21. Amat rolls Meditation/Void: 6k4 = 16. Akiko wins! She learns two of Amat's traits or skills. Akiko examines her opponent carefully and learns that Amat has Agility 4 and Intelligence 3. Amat then declares she will not use Endure or Dodge in the next round.

Round 2 - Preparation Phase

Action Selection: Akiko chooses Readiness. Amat chooses Readiness.

Resolution: Akiko rolls Kenjutsu/Fire: 8k3 = 20. Amat rolls Kenjutsu/Fire: 8k3 = 20. This is essentially a tie—both are master swordswomen. Checking the elemental cycle: Fire vs. Fire is a complete tie. Checking Ring (Fire vs. Fire), Skill (Kenjutsu vs. Kenjutsu), and Insight Rank, they are identical. This is a true tie, and neither receives their Readiness bonus.

Withdrawal Phase

Both duelists stand firm. There will be no withdrawal. This will be settled only by blood and death.

Striking Phase - Initiative Round

Akiko rolls Iaijutsu/Reflexes: 8k3 = 18. Amat rolls Iaijutsu/Reflexes: 8k4 = 17. Difference is 1 (<5), so this is a Kharmic Strike!

Striking Phase - Attack Round (Simultaneous)

Amat's Attack: Amat rolls Kenjutsu/Agility: 8k4 = 17, beats Akiko's armor TN of 15. She hits! Damage: 9k2 = 24 wounds. A blow across Akiko's ribs.

Akiko's Counter-Attack: Akiko rolls Kenjutsu/Agility: 8k3 = 26, beats Amat's armor TN of 15. She hits! Damage: 9k3 = 71 wounds from multiple explodes. The blow is absolutely devastating.

Both weapons find their mark. But Akiko's counter-strike is far superior. Her blade finds Amat's heart, and the Unicorn duelist staggers backward, her life draining away in moments. Amat falls, her jade katana clattering to the stone floor.

Akiko, though wounded (24 wounds, roughly equal to a Grazed wound level with moderate additional damage), straightens herself. The pain is not enough for her to cry out and her Crane discipline holds. She stands victorious, her honor restored.

Example 4: Shiba Tsune vs Hida Yuki - Duel to the Death

Setup: In a field outside a small village, Shiba Tsune, a phoenix bushi, faces Hida Yuki, a grizzled Crab berserker. The dispute originated from conflicting interpretations of religious law, but pride and stubbornness have driven it to steel. Yuki, confident in Crab toughness and directness, believes his straightforward approach will overcome the Phoenix's esoteric techniques. Tsune, however, is no mere bushi—he is a trained warrior with years of blade work behind him.

Round 1 - Preparation Phase

Action Selection: Yuki chooses Assess. Tsune chooses Assess.

Resolution: Yuki rolls Battle/Water: 5k3 = 16. Tsune rolls Battle/Water: 5k3 = 14. Yuki wins! He learns two of Tsune's traits or skills: he discovers his Iaijutsu 4 and Kenjutsu 3. Tsune then declares he will NOT use Focus or Dodge in the next round.

Round 2 - Preparation Phase

Action Selection: Yuki chooses Dodge. Tsune chooses Readiness.

Resolution: Yuki rolls Defense/Air: 4k2 = 9. Tsune rolls Kenjutsu/Fire: 6k3 = 18. Tsune wins decisively! He gains +5 to his hit roll, while Yuki's defensive posture fails to provide him any benefit.

Withdrawal Phase

Neither duelist gives ground. The Crab, despite his knowledge of Tsune's skills, refuses to yield. Both are committed to this contest.

Striking Phase - Initiative Round

Tsune rolls Iaijutsu/Reflexes: 5k3 = 18. Yuki rolls Iaijutsu/Reflexes: 4k3 = 13. Difference is 5 (≥5), so Tsune strikes first.

Striking Phase - Attack Round

Tsune's Attack: Tsune rolls Kenjutsu/Agility + 5 (Readiness bonus): 6k3 + 5 = 24, well above Yuki's armor TN of 15. He hits with precision! Damage: 5k3 = 16 wounds. A deep, grievous cut across Yuki's shoulder and down his back. Yuki screams—the pain is immense, but it hardens his resolve rather than weakening it.

Yuki's Counter-Attack: Despite his wounds, the Crab warrior attacks back. He rolls Kenjutsu/Agility: 4k2 = 9, which fails to beat Tsune's armor TN of 12. He misses! His blade swings wildly from pain and desperation, but Tsune's phoenix training allows him to slip aside.

Transition to Skirmish

This duel to the death has ended in a strike exchange with only one drawing blood. However, the damage is not fatal. The fight transitions to a full skirmish as both warriors circle each other, seeking the killing blow.

Skirmish Round Initiative: Yuki rolls his Initiative: 3k3 = 11. Tsune rolls his Initiative: 4k2 = 10. Yuki wins initiative! He attacks first in this round.

Yuki's Skirmish Attack: Driven by his wounds and rage, Yuki rolls Kenjutsu/Agility: 5k3 = 17, beating Tsune's armor TN of 15. He hits! Damage: 7k2 = 49 wounds from multiple explosions. A powerful, anguished blow that takes Tsune across the chest.

Tsune's Counter-Attack: Though wounded, Tsune attempts to strike back. He rolls Kenjutsu/Agility: 6k3 - 20 (Crippled) = -5, which fails to beat Yuki's armor TN of 15. He misses! His blade failing him as the blood starts to run down his face.

Yuki attacks again. Seeing a dazed Tsune he goes for the kill. He rolls Kenjutsu/Agility: 5k3 = 19, a masterful strike. Damage: 7k2 = 28 wounds. The blow is devastating, and Shiba Tsune staggers and falls to one knee. His blood pours freely as his vision darkens.

Tsune cannot continue. The Phoenix warrior, despite his skill, has been overwhelmed by the raw power and tenacity of the Crab.

Conclusion

Hida Yuki is victorious! Tsune's esoteric training and keen mind could not overcome the Crab's sheer toughness and determination. Yuki stands bloodied but triumphant. The lesson is clear: sometimes straightforward steel and stubborn will are worth more than technique.

Example 5: Kakita Toshimoko (in Disguise) vs Mirumoto Sasuke - Non-Lethal Duel

Setup: In a hidden dojo far from the courts, a mysterious duelist known only as "the Grey Wanderer" has been challenged by Mirumoto Sasuke, a dragon duelist of considerable skill. Sasuke, confident and young, believes he can easily overcome any wanderer. He does not know he is facing Kakita Toshimoko, a legendary swordmaster.

Round 1 - Preparation Phase

Action Selection: Toshimoko chooses Assess. Sasuke chooses Assess.

Resolution: Toshimoko rolls Battle/Water: 9k4 = 32. Sasuke rolls Battle/Water: 5k3 = 15. Toshimoko crushes his opponent! He learns two of Sasuke's traits or skills: Toshimoko observes Sasuke's Iaijutsu 4 and Void 3. Sasuke, unsettled by the overwhelming roll, declares he will NOT use Endure or Dodge in the next round. Sasuke suspects something is very wrong.

Round 2 - Preparation Phase

Action Selection: Toshimoko chooses Focus. Sasuke chooses Focus.

Resolution: Toshimoko rolls Meditation/Void: 10k5 = 52. Sasuke rolls Meditation/Void: 8k3 = 40. Toshimoko destroys his opponent once again! The gap is so enormous that Sasuke's blood runs cold. This is not a normal duelist. This is a master.

Withdrawal Phase

Sasuke stares at his opponent. The "Grey Wanderer's" skill is utterly transcendent. The dragon warrior realizes, with sudden clarity, that he is outmatched beyond measure. Every fiber of his being screams that continuing this duel would be not merely losing, but humiliation.

Mirumoto Sasuke takes a deep breath and steps back, bowing deeply and sincerely. "Master," he says, his voice steady but respectful, "I withdraw from this duel. I concede defeat to one clearly superior to me in every way."

Toshimoko nods slowly and withdraws silently from the Dojo.

Conclusion

Mirumoto Sasuke has withdrawn, and thus loses the duel. More significantly, his name will be associated with withdrawing from a duel, which costs him Fame—a significant blow to his reputation. However, he gains Honor for recognizing a superior opponent and withdrawing. The dragon warrior receives wisdom and humility as his reward. When word spreads that he faced Kakita Toshimoko himself, his reputation recovers somewhat, though the stigma of withdrawal lingers. But in the courts of Rokugan, those who know the truth respect Sasuke's decision—only a fool fights a master merely for pride.
