Mounts

This section covers the rules and guidelines for mounts in the game.

Mounts and Rokugan

Until the return of the Unicorn in the 800s, Rokugan's use of mounts was limited to the native Rokugani Pony, which was unsuitable for warfare due to its size. Its primary use was for travel, communication, scouting and light mounted infantry, which would dismount from their pony once arriving at the battle.

The Unicorn's return changed all of Rokugan's approach to mounted warfare, introducing new breeds of warhorses and cavalry tactics that significantly enhanced the effectiveness of mounted units on the battlefield. Below will be an overview of how mounts are used in the game.

Game play

This section details the rules of using mounts. It is important to note that a mount counts as a fully playable character that follows the rules of the game, including taking actions, rolling dice, using skills, and being subject to damage and other effects.

Experience points

Mounts do not generally gain experience points and as such cannot purchase new skills or increase traits beyond the ones they have. GMs may of course relax this rule at their discretion.

Wound Ranks

Similar to other creatures, Mounts have simpler wound ranks than characters as will be covered below.

Commanding Mounts

Mounts cannot act independently and must be given a command to take action. This will always involve the use of a Free Action by the one attempting to control the mount, regardless of the action the mount is being instructed to perform. Under normal circumstances where a mount belongs to the owner, it will follow the commands. Otherwise though, Horsemanship rolls may be required to successfully command the mount.

Unless indicated otherwise, once instructed the mount will continue to carry out the action until it is completed or until it is given a new command. See the Combat section below for more details on actions in combat.

Combat

This section details special rules for handling mounts in combat.

Initiative

When rolling for Initiative, both the Mount and the Rider roll separately and the lower of the results is used to determine the initiative order for the pair.

Actions

To have the mount take its turn in combat, the rider must use a Free Action to command the mount. The mount will then take its full set of Actions for the Round, and once done the rider may then continue to take their remaining actions.

This means that a mount may not be able to act if the rider uses up all of his actions on other things, however if the Rider's is at Wound Rank: Down, then the mount may flee to safety with or without its rider.
