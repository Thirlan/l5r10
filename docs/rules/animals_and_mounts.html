Animals and Mounts

This section covers the rules and guidelines for interacting with natural (i.e. non-magical) animals and the special case of mounts in the game.

Game mechanics

Animals follow the same basic rules as other creatures in the game as covered by the monsters section of the rules.

Interacting with animals

The level of domestication of an animal strongly affects what game mechanics are used to interact with them and can be broken down into to simple categories, wild and trained animals.

Wild animals are those that have not been domesticated or trained to follow commands. They act according to their instincts and natural behaviors, making interactions with them unpredictable and potentially dangerous.

Trained animals are those that have undergone both domestication and training to follow specific commands and perform certain roles. Such training will be covered in this section below.

Interacting with wild animals

Any attempt to communicate, influence or request anything from a wild animal is done by the handle animal skill using a complex action. The use of magical spells or abilities to communicate with them does not remove the need to roll the appropriate handle animal check, but does provide a +5 TR bonus on the handle animal skill roll. Being able to communicate does not mean you are knowledgeable with how to behave or interact with the animal.

Interacting with trained animals

Animals can be trained in specific roles, which will be covered below. Commanding an animal to fufill a specific role does not require a handle animal skill roll and instead requires a free action, as long as the one issuing the command:

  • Has at least 1 skill rank in Handle Animal for the appropriate animal
  • OR has at least 1 rank in Horsemanship if the animal is trained as a mount
  • AND is considered friendly by the animal

All actions outside of the specific role still require a handle animal skill roll using a complex action.

Unless the animal has the Intelligent trait, it cannot act independently, other than following basic instincts, and must always be given a command to take an action requested of it.

Training Animals

Training, is a long and arduous process requiring a year, often starting at birth. The table below provides exmaples of animals that can be trained for specific tasks, such as hunting, scouting, tracking, performance, mounts and so on forth. Training animals in a role can grant them skills.

The process can be summarized in the following steps:

  • Choose the animal to be trained.
  • Acquire an infant of the animal.
    • Using a Commerce roll to find a seller and then buy is the simplest process but other methods such as breeding or capturing in the wild are all possible.
  • Determine one or more roles for which the animal will be trained.
  • Spend required number of years training the animal. Spending at least 3 uninterrupted hours a day throughout that period.
  • Spend the cost to train the animal for the full duration of the training period.
  • At the end of the training period, make a handle animal skill roll against the TN to train, plus all the modifiers for each role chosen.
  • If the roll:
    • Succeeds: The animal is trained for the specified role(s).
    • Fails: The animal is not trained and is no longer considered suitable for further training, having either developed behavioral issues or failed to bond with the trainer. It is considered domesticated and can be resold at 25% of its purchase cost as a pet or for other non-specialized purposes.

The table below can be read as follows:

  • Animal: The type of animal being trained.
  • TN to train: The target number required to successfully train the animal. This roll is made at the end the training period.
  • Years to train: The number of years it typically takes to train the animal.
  • Cost to purchase (Koku): The cost to purchase the animal.
  • Cost to train (Koku): The cost to train the animal for the full duration of the training. The cost includes food, equipment, and any other necessary expenses.
  • Affinity to roles: The roles the animal is naturally inclined or suitable for. It can take one of these roles at no increase to the training TN.
  • Forbidden roles: The roles the animal cannot be trained for.

Mounts and Rokugan

Until the return of the Unicorn in the 800s, Rokugan's use of mounts was limited to the native Rokugani Pony, which was unsuitable for warfare due to its size. Its primary use was for travel, communication, scouting and light mounted infantry, which would dismount from their pony once arriving at the battle.

The Unicorn's return changed all of Rokugan's approach to mounted warfare, introducing new breeds of warhorses and cavalry tactics that significantly enhanced the effectiveness of mounted units on the battlefield. Below will be an overview of how mounts are used in the game.

Movement and Mounts

Mounted characters have specific rules for movement while on their mount:

  • Getting onto or off of a mount:
    • Stationary: simple action
    • Moving: horsemanship roll TN 25, or suffer 2k2 physical DR from the fall and suffer the Conditional Effect: Prone
  • Characters cannot use their actions to make move actions while mounted
  • Characters can only move with their mount's move actions

Combat

This section details special rules for handling animals in combat.

Bringing into Combat

Bringing an animal into combat requires either an animal handling roll, or a horsemanship roll if mounted, against a TN of 20. This is only done once at the start of combat. Failure means the animal is not available as it temporarily flees to safety.

Initiative

Characters that bring animals into combat always roll both the animal's and character's initiative, keeping the lowest. Both will act using the same initiative order. In the case of multiple animals, roll for each.

This both simplifies the initiative process and represents the coordination required between the animal and the handler.

Maneuvers

Unless otherwise stated in the animal's stats, it may not perform any maneuvers in combat.

Actions

To have the animal take its turn in combat, the animal handler must use a Free Action to coordinate/command it, regardless of whether it has the Intelligent trait or not. The animal may then take its full set of Actions for the Round, and once done the animal handler may then continue to take their remaining actions.

This means that an animal may not be able to act if the animal handler uses up all of their actions on other things, however if the animal handler is at unconscious or dead for whatever reason, then:

  • If the animal has the Intelligent trait, it may continue to act
  • If the animal is a mount, it flees with its rider
  • Otherwise, it will move towards its handler and attack any threats that attack their handler
