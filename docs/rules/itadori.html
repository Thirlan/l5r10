Itadori Changes

Below are some of the fundamental changes to the original game mechanics under the Itadori ruleset.

+# TR / -# TR

Problem: there was no quick keyword to explain that a bonus was applied to your roll instead of the TN. This compounds with the clean up of "Free Raises" mentioend below.

+# TR / -# TR indicates a bonus To Your Roll (TR), so -5 TR means a -5 penalty to your roll and a +5 TR means a +5 bonus to your roll.

Raises and Free Raises

Problem: Raises and Free Raises were confusing and unintuitive when applying to contested and non-contested rolls.

"Do I get a bonus to my rolls with a Free Raise? But I thought Raises increased the TN? Does my opponent get a bonus then to their roll because I made a Free Raise?"

Raises are now clarified to always mean a -5 TR to YOU roll score and Free Raises are purged from all documents, being replaced with a simple +5 TR

Emphasis

Problem: Emphasis on skills Athletics(Running) or Courtier(Manipulation) added unnecessary layers of complexity. The bonus of buying an emphasis to re-roll 1s was also too weak as the power was in some cases too specific.

Emphasis has been removed from skills and as a game term, but re-rolls have been kept and re-purposed for entire skills, improving is value and then given to certain underpowered schools.

Squares / Feet

Problem: The original game only provided feet for measurements, which resulted in extra work on the player's part to transform this onto a grid measured in squares for most table top play. This was extra painful for those who were not familiar with the Imperial measurement system.

All Feet measurements will have a square measurement in the form of # sq / #'. Example 1 sq / 5', where 1 square is always assumed to be 5' x 5'.

Bunpitsu (Calligraphy + Forgery)

Problem: Too many little skills in general made the game difficult to remember and manage. Calligraphy was an underused skill and Forgery overlapped enough with it to justify combining them.

Calligraphy and Forgery have been combined into a single skill called Bunpitsu (文筆, scribecraft). This skill encompasses both the refined art of calligraphy used for courtly beauty and encryption, as well as the precise forgery of documents. Bunpitsu uses Intelligence as its primary trait (adjusted from Forgery's Agility) to better reflect the intellectual discipline required for both calligraphy and successful document falsification. The skill includes Cipher and Forgery Emphases for specialized applications.

Political Skills and Resources

The political rules now define Courtier, Etiquette, Intimidation, Sincerity, and Temptation as the core political skills, with Investigation as a supporting skill. Their pairings have been clarified so the resources used by political actions are explicit: Courtier uses Awareness and Fame for soft power, Etiquette uses Awareness and Status when acting, Intimidation uses Awareness and Status for hard power, and Sincerity and defensive Etiquette use Willpower and Honor.

Investigation is split by approach: Perception is used for physical evidence, while Awareness is used for social information, lies, emotions, and hidden motives. Investigation does not receive Status or Fame. Political skill entries now use the same table format as Bunpitsu and include example usages and opposed rolls.

Bunpitsu also supports formal correspondence, forgery, and written ciphers. The Ango Advantage allows coded communication; written ciphers use Bunpitsu / Agility, while verbal and visual ciphers use Courtier / Awareness.

Han-Kyu, Yumi and Dai-Kyu

Problem: the original game misunderstood that there are only two bow types in Feudal Japan, the Han-Kyu and the Dai-Kyu. The Dai-Kyu could be used both on foot and on horseback. The Yumi is the general term for bow, of which Han-Kyu and Dai-Kyu are specific types.

In the spirit of simplifying the rules the Yumi was removed and the Dai-Kyu was simplified to be usable both on Horseback and on foot.
